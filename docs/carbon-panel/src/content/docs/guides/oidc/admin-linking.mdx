Use this flow to sign in with SSO on an existing local admin account while
keeping its local break-glass access.

## Prerequisites

- Configure OIDC and verify that discovery and ID-token validation work.
- Sign in locally as an active admin with a locally assigned `admin` grant.
  API tokens and SSO sessions cannot start a link.
- Use `role_mapping` to map the provider group to each role that should be
  available through SSO. For example, map `carbon-admins` to `admin` after
  reviewing who belongs to that group.

## Link your account

<Steps>
1. Open **Settings → Auth → OIDC / Single Sign-On** and select **Link SSO account**.
2. Sign in at the identity provider using the account you intend to link.
   Complete the flow within five minutes in the same browser. Keep the
   initiating local session active until you return to Settings.
3. Confirm the success message. Sign out and use **Sign in with SSO** to
   verify the linked account and its mapped roles.
</Steps>

The link binds the issuer and subject from the verified ID token to your
existing account. Your password and local grants stay available. Local
sign-ins use locally owned roles, and SSO sign-ins use provider-mapped roles.
Removing a mapped group revokes its SSO grant at the next verified sign-in.
In **Settings → Users**, edit locally managed roles and review SSO-managed
roles separately. Manage provider grants through the identity provider.

## If linking fails

| Result | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Link expired or no longer valid | Sign in locally and start again from Settings. Another sign-in, a restart, or an expired session can cancel a pending link. |
| Identity requires review | The identity is already linked, or the subject matches a record under another or unknown issuer. Review the stored identity and use the documented recovery path. |
| Role conflict | Check the explicit role mapping and existing role grants before retrying. |
| Provider declined the request | Check account selection and the provider's app assignment or sign-in policy. |

Audit records contain an issuer and, after identity verification, a subject
hash. Keep browser link URLs, authorization codes and tokens out of logs
and support tickets.
